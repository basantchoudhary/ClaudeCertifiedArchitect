CCA-F — Mock Exam #7 (Exam Level)

6 scenario item-sets · 60 questions · 120-minute timer · every item has a runner-up · answer shown on click (exam mode optional)

Easy to read, hard to choose

This paper is pitched at the level of the real exam as candidates describe it: the questions are easy to understand, and the hard part is choosing between two answers that both look right.

  • Short, plain questions. About 40–80 words, one problem each, with the deciding fact stated plainly, usually just before the question.
  • One step of reasoning. If you know the exam-guide point behind the deciding fact, you can get to the answer.
  • Mainstream topics. The guide's most-tested points, not edge-case features.
  • No clear winner. Every question has a runner-up that is genuinely good practice, and the options are length-matched with no giveaways.

Harder than this, Mock #6 makes you work harder to read the question. Use #7 to build the skill the real exam actually tests, then #6 as a stretch.

How this paper is built

  • Every question has a runner-up — an option that would be correct if one fact in the question were different. That fact is stated once, in the middle of the question, and not highlighted.
  • All options are about the same length, and the right answer is rarely the longest. This is checked automatically on every question.
  • Options state an action, never a reason. Wording like "so that", "because", "always" or "never" does not appear in any option.
  • Topic tags stay hidden until the question is graded.

Use it to learn, not just to test. Every question has the same six steps, shown as a strip under it:

  1. UnderstandBefore answering: the question in plain words and the sentence that holds the clue.
  2. AnswerPick an option. In practice mode it is graded straight away.
  3. WhyRight or wrong, which one was the runner-up, and why each option wins or loses.
  4. Think it throughThe premise to correct, a mind map (what you see → where the fault is → the fix), the key sentences quoted, the rule to remember.
  5. See it runA simulator: apply any option to the system and watch the requests, tools, hooks and result step by step. Your pick is preselected.
  6. What ifThe same question with the deciding fact changed. The runner-up wins, so you see exactly which fact decided it.

When an answer is revealed you see the deciding fact, and the runner-up is marked in amber. The final report tells you how many of your wrong answers were the runner-up. If that is most of them, you know the material and the gap is in how you read the question. Before choosing, find the sentence that rules out the second-best option.
