# Beat sheet: "Several customer matches" (about 8–10 minutes)

Not a script. Glance at the point, then say it in your own words while showing the page.
Record one beat per take, and pause 2 seconds before restarting a sentence; the fillers get cut in editing.

## 1. The hook (30 s)
- Show the title and the At a glance box.
- Say: the lookup finds more than one customer. Every wrong option sounds sensible: the newest account, the closest name, a person, a list. Only one of them is the exam answer.
- Promise: one question tells you which side you're on.

## 2. The pharmacist (1 min)
- Scroll to the two boxes. Three patients called Priya Sharma.
- The guess: hand the bag to the most recent Priya. Right most days; the day it's wrong, someone leaves with another person's medicine.
- The question: "date of birth?" Ten seconds, one record fits.
- Define "identifier" in one line: a detail only one record has (order number, postcode).

## 3. The diagram (1 min)
- Trace it: message, then the get_customer search, then "how many match?".
- One match: go ahead. Two or more: the policy decides. Read the four boxes and the line under each.
- Point out that "hand to a person" is right but slow, and "list the matches" is right but leaks.

## 4. The one question (1 min)
- Butter box: "Does the agent know for certain which record is meant, or would it be picking one?"
- Read the four bullets. Stress the third: if the customer won't give an identifier, the agent can't make progress, and that IS a trigger for a person.
- Word lists: "two accounts match", "same name", "picks the one with the latest order" vs "one match", "order number given".

## 5. Simulator (3 min). The heart of the video
- One match + any policy: 8 of 8 right, verdict "Fine here". "With one match there's nothing to choose. The flaws only show with several."
- Three Priyas + pick the most recent: 3 of 8 right, 5 wrong people. Scroll the pink cards: "the newest box belongs to only one of three Priyas".
- Three Priyas + best name match: 5 of 8 right, 3 wrong. Show chat 3 ("I'm in Leeds", but there are two Leeds Priyas) and chat 6 (an old Bristol address). "Closer, but still a guess."
- Three Priyas + ask for an identifier: 8 of 8 right, 9 extra questions, 1 handed to a person. Open chat 7: she won't give a detail, the agent asks once more, then hands over because it can't make progress. Verdict "Best fit (exam answer)".
- Three Priyas + escalate to a human: 8 of 8 right, but 8 handovers, about 120 minutes of waiting. Verdict "Overbuilt".
- Three Priyas + list the matches: 8 of 8 right, 16 other customers' details shown. Verdict "Leaks data".
- Shared email + most recent: 4 of 8, every Rahul chat changed Priya's account. Then ask: 8 of 8, 8 extra questions.
- Say once: the chats and the 15-minute wait are illustrative; the shape is the exam guide's.

## 6. The four traps table (1 min)
- Match each row to a run you just did: most recent, best name match, escalate, list.
- Land the overbuilt one: several matches is not one of the three escalation triggers.

## 7. Practice question 1, live (2 min)
- Read the stem. Point at "returns the one with the latest box" and "a refund goes to a stranger's card".
- Rule out options by naming their trap (heuristic guess, overbuilt, leaks data). Pick the identifier answer, open "Think it through".
- Leave question 2 (same trap, other wording) and question 3 (when a person really is the answer: the customer asks for one) for the viewer.

## 8. Close (20 s)
- Rule to remember: several matches means ask, never guess.
- Read the exam guide line from the butter box. Point back to the Trap gym page.
