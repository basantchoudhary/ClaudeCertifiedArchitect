# Beat sheet: "Confidence and sampling" (about 8–10 minutes)

Not a script. Glance at the point, then say it in your own words while showing the page.
Record one beat per take, and pause 2 seconds before restarting a sentence; the fillers get cut in editing.

## 1. The hook (30 s)
- Show the title and the At a glance box.
- Say: 97% accuracy, and the team wants to switch off review. Every wrong option trusts something that sounds like evidence: the average, a random sample, Claude's own confidence.
- Promise: one question exposes all three.

## 2. The school average (1 min)
- Scroll to the two boxes. The school averages 85%; one class is at 40%.
- The average barely moves, because hundreds of other pupils do well. Only checking class by class shows it.
- Define two terms from the paragraph: stratified random sampling (a random sample from each group) and calibrated confidence (a score checked against a labelled set, so 90% sure means right 90% of the time).

## 3. The diagram (1 min)
- Trace it: documents, Claude extracts four fields, route each field: posts by itself, or a person checks.
- The five checks below all feed that routing decision. Read the line under each: one number; rare types barely appear; never checked; each type and field measured; routes each field.

## 4. The one question (1 min)
- Butter box: "Has accuracy been checked for each document type and field, or only as one overall number?"
- Read the four bullets. Stress the last: after launch, keep sampling the confident, auto-posted fields.
- Word lists: "97% overall", "handwritten forms", "only a handful were in the set" vs "which fields to review", "labelled set", "calibrate".

## 5. Simulator (3 min). The heart of the video
- Setup: 10,000 documents a month, four fields, handwritten forms only 2%. Every method gets the same budget: 400 labelled documents. A field posts by itself only at 97% or above.
- Overall accuracy only: one number, 97.3%. 1,077 wrong values a month post unseen. Press "Show what is really true": handwritten Amount 64%, Ref. 56%. Verdict "Masks weak spots".
- A plain random sample: the handwritten row has 4 documents, and by luck they look fine (100%, 100%, 100%, 75%). "Too few", so they post anyway. Scanned PDFs got 65 documents, enough to catch most fields. 829 wrong a month, 11% of fields checked. Verdict "Too thin".
- Claude rates its own confidence: no grid at all. 596 wrong a month, only 3% checked. "Confident misreadings of handwriting went straight through." Verdict "Uncalibrated".
- Stratified by type: 100 of each type. Handwritten and scanned rows go pink, people check them. 545 wrong a month, 17% checked. Point at the truth grid: invoices and receipts still post 545 errors. Verdict "Half the answer".
- Stratified + calibrated field confidence: 86 wrong a month, 8% checked, error rate among posted fields 0.2%. Point at the per-cell "% checked": handwritten Ref. 100%, invoices 4–5%. Verdict "Best fit (exam answer)".
- Say once: the accuracies and catch rates are illustrative; the samples use a fixed seed, so the grid is the same every time.

## 6. The four traps table (1 min)
- Match each row to a run: overall only, random sample, low-confidence only, Claude rates itself.
- Land it: confidence routing is right only once it is calibrated on labelled data.

## 7. Practice question 1, live (2 min)
- Read the stem. Point at "97% field accuracy", "only a handful were in the set", "most errors were in limits on those schedules".
- Rule out options by naming their trap: too late (ongoing random sample), too early (calibrate before the labels cover schedules), different problem (two runs agreeing).
- Pick C, open "Think it through", show the mind map.
- Leave questions 2 (two steps: stratify and calibrate) and 3 (only 20 redelivery cases in the audit) for the viewer.

## 8. Close (20 s)
- Rule to remember: don't trust the average; check each type and field before you automate.
- Read the three exam guide lines from the butter box. Point back to the Trap gym page.
