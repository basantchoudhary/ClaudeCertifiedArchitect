# Beat sheet: "Resume, fork or start fresh?" (about 8–10 minutes)

Not a script. Glance at the point, then say it in your own words while showing the page.
Record one beat per take, and pause 2 seconds before restarting a sentence; the fillers get cut in editing.

## 1. The hook (30 s)
- Show the title and the At a glance box.
- Say: you come back to work Claude started yesterday. Every option on the exam is a real way to carry on. The trap is using the one that fits a different situation.

## 2. Bookmark, photocopy, fresh notes (1 min)
- Scroll to the three boxes. Bookmark: nobody touched the book. Photocopy: two notes on the same page. Fresh notes: someone rewrote three chapters.
- Land the key idea: tool results are snapshots. They show the files as they were when Claude read them, and they don't update.
- Map the three: --resume is the bookmark, fork_session the photocopy, a new session with a structured summary the fresh notes. "Structured" means headed sections, not prose.

## 3. The diagram (1 min)
- Trace it left to right: yesterday's session, the overnight marker, then the four ways to carry on.
- Point at "+ tell it which files changed" inside the resume box: the guide's middle way.
- Read the caption: snapshots.

## 4. The one question (1 min)
- Butter box: "Is what the old session saw still true, and do you want one path or two?"
- Two questions, three answers. Read the three word lists fast: "nothing has changed", "try two approaches", "a teammate merged".

## 5. Simulator (3 min). The heart of the video
- Teammate changed 3 files + --resume. Point at the three pink rows: round_cents() was renamed, the discount order flipped, tax is now per region. Run: 56 min, 33 min of rework. Verdict "Stale results": each stale snapshot became a failed edit.
- Same situation + resume and tell it which files changed: rows turn butter, "old and new copy". 27 min, 5 min rework when an answer mixes in round_cents() from the old copy. Verdict "Also works": the guide endorses it; with one small change it would be the best fit.
- + New session with a structured summary: three rows "read again today", three "summary only", no stale rows. 26 min. Verdict "Best fit (exam answer)".
- + fork_session: "Copies the problem". Two branches, wrong in the same way, 40 min of rework.
- Now the trap in reverse: nothing changed + new session from scratch. 1 h 05 min against 16 min for --resume. Verdict "Wastes work".
- Two approaches + --resume: last row turns pink, "carried over". B copies A's credit-note workaround. Verdict "Polluted context". Then fork_session: "kept apart", 39 min, Best fit.
- Say once: the minutes are illustrative; the shape is what the exam guide says.

## 6. The three traps table (1 min)
- Match each trap to a run you just did: stale results (changed + resume), wastes work (nothing changed + scratch), polluted context (two approaches + resume).

## 7. Practice question 1, live (2 min)
- Read the stem. Point at "Nobody has changed the billing code since then" and "carry on that same investigation".
- Rule out options by their trap: loses detail (summary), redoes the work (trace again), overbuilt (fork).
- Click C, open "Think it through".
- Leave question 2 (fork for two migrations) and question 3 (corrected papers, fresh session) for the viewer: "pause and try them".

## 8. Close (20 s)
- Rule to remember: same world, resume; two paths, fork; changed world, start fresh with a summary.
- Point back to the Trap gym page for the next trap.
