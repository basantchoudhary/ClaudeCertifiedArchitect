# Beat sheet: "Sources and conflicts" (about 8–10 minutes)

Not a script. Glance at the point, then say it in your own words while showing the page.
Record one beat per take, and pause 2 seconds before restarting a sentence; the fillers get cut in editing.

## 1. The hook (30 s)
- Show the page title.
- Say: every option in these questions produces a tidy report. The trap is a report you can't check, or one that settles a disagreement by quietly picking a side.
- Promise: two things to keep, the source on every claim and the date on every figure.

## 2. Two newspapers and a rally (1 min)
- Good reporter: "Police said 8,000; the organisers said 20,000." Careless reporter: "about 14,000", a number nobody gave.
- And 8,000 in 2019 versus 20,000 in 2024 is not a disagreement. It's two years.
- Define once: synthesis = the step that merges findings into one story. Claim-source mapping = each finding travels as data: the claim, its source, its date.

## 3. The diagram (1 min)
- Top path: prose summary, synthesis compresses it, "sources drop out", report you can't check.
- Bottom path: claim, source, date as data, synthesis "keeps the links, compares dates", report with a table and cited prose.
- Bottom row: two figures disagree? Pick one and the other vanishes; show both, each with source and date.

## 4. The one question (1 min)
- Butter box: "Can a reader still tell which source each claim came from, and are disagreeing figures shown honestly?"
- The two word lists: "lost track of which source", "cited the wrong paper" versus "two reports disagree", "published in different years".

## 5. Simulator (3 min). The heart of the video
- Different figures + plain summary: "about 35%". Point at the "invented figure" chip. 0 of 2 lines checkable. Verdict Breaks. "35% appears in neither source."
- Different figures + keep the most authoritative: 2 of 2 checkable, but figures dropped: 1. Verdict Picks one. "Every line checks out, and 41% is gone."
- Different figures + claim-source mapping: the table with 28% (NSO survey) and 41% (Chamber poll), the note that they measure different things, 4 of 4 checkable. Best fit (exam answer). "The reader weighs them; the system didn't pick."
- Different figures + show side by side: Also works for the conflict, but the café line says "no source": 2 of 3. "Only the numbers carried attribution."
- Different years + plain summary: "vary widely, from 9% to 28%", chip "time read as conflict". Breaks.
- Different years + show side by side: the dates are right there in the table, and the report still says "Sources disagree". Verdict Misreads time: "Dates must be compared, not only shown."
- Different years + claim-source mapping: "rose from 9% in 2019 to 28% in 2023", same survey, two years. Best fit.
- Sources agree + keep the most authoritative: Also works. "When they agree, dropping one costs little. The trap only bites when they don't."
- Point at "Figures shown as": table for the mapping, prose for the plain summary. The guide wants figures as tables, news as prose.
- Say once: the town and figures are made up; the shape is what the exam guide says.

## 6. The four traps table (1 min)
- Read the trap names and match each to a run: picks one (different figures + keep the most authoritative), invents a figure (plain summary, 35%), too late (citations added at the end), loses provenance (prose summaries from subagents).

## 7. Practice question 1, live (2 min)
- Read the stem. Point at "The synthesis subagent turns the summaries into flowing prose" and "cites each sentence by finding the summary that reads most like it".
- Rule out options by naming their trap: A "treats the symptom", B "only usually works", C "still guessing".
- Click D, open "Think it through", show the mind map: where does the link break? At synthesis.
- Leave question 2 (the committee needs both bleeding-rate figures) and question 3 (4% and 9%, a decade apart) for the viewer: "pause and try them".

## 8. Close (20 s)
- Rule to remember: every claim keeps its source and date; conflicts are shown, not settled by picking one.
- Point back to the Trap gym page for the next trap.
