Why this exam is different from Mocks #1–#3

Mocks #1–#3 test whether you can recognise the right concept. Their stems average about 100–160 characters and each has one clearly-correct option beside three throwaways. That is why a high score on them doesn't predict much: you were pattern-matching, not deciding.

This exam tests whether you can choose between four defensible options. Every item obeys four rules:

  • The stem carries real system state and numbers — what was already tried, and what it cost.
  • All four options are things a competent architect might actually do. Usually two are genuinely good ideas.
  • The discriminator sits in the qualifier — first step, guarantees, root cause, least added complexity. Read it before you read the options.
  • Every option carries its own rationale on reveal, so a wrong pick teaches you where the boundary actually is.

Each wrong answer is also tagged with the trap pattern it belongs to. At the end you get a domain breakdown plus a ranked list of which traps caught you — that list is your revision plan.
