CCA-F — Mock Exam #4 (Real-Exam Style)

6 scenario item-sets · 60 questions · 120-minute timer · every item has a runner-up · answer shown on click (exam mode optional)

Rebuilt to match the real exam

This paper was rebuilt in October 2026 after its first version proved easier than the real exam. In the original, always picking the longest option scored 96%, the correct option was the only one that explained itself, and several wrong options could be ruled out without knowing the material. Each question still tests the same idea as before, but the stem and options have been rewritten to the Mock #6 standard. Picking the longest option now scores 18%, below the 25% you would get by chance.

The previous version is preserved in this folder (parts/original-questions.js) and in git history.

How this paper is built

  • Every question has a runner-up — an option that would be correct if one fact in the question were different. That fact is stated once, in the middle of the question, and not highlighted.
  • All options are about the same length, and the right answer is rarely the longest. This is checked automatically on every question.
  • Options state an action, never a reason. Wording like "so that", "because", "always" or "never" does not appear in any option.
  • Topic tags stay hidden until the question is graded.

Use it to learn, not just to test. Under every question there is an Explain this question simply link: open it before you answer to see, in everyday words, what is being asked and which sentence holds the clue. After you answer, the plain-language explanation comes first: why the right answer is right, why each wrong one is wrong, and a one-line rule to remember. Then a Think it through section works the question the way an examiner would: the assumption that makes it confusing, a mind map of the whole topic (what you observe → where the fault is → the fix), each key sentence of the question quoted with what it proves, and the exam-guide line it comes from. The technical version is folded underneath.

When an answer is revealed you see the deciding fact, and the runner-up is marked in amber. The final report tells you how many of your wrong answers were the runner-up. If that is most of them, you know the material and the gap is in how you read the question. Before choosing, find the sentence that rules out the second-best option.
