Why this paper is harder than #4 and #5

Mocks #4 and #5 felt exam-grade but were easier than the real exam, in ways you could pick up without noticing:

  • The right answer was usually the longest. In Mock #5 it was the longest option in 52 of 60 questions.
  • The right answer explained itself. Only the correct option said why it worked ("…which is the field that actually reports…"). The others just stated an action.
  • Wrong answers had giveaways. "Restate the rule in capitals", "fine-tune the model", "remove the tool" — options you could rule out without knowing the material.
  • The topic was printed on every question. A tag like "1.4 Prerequisite gates" told you the expected answer area.

The real exam does none of this. Two of its options are usually both good practice. What separates them is one plain fact in the question: whether the check blocks a merge, whether contractors need it on clone, whether the value is actually in the document, whether you were asked for a first step or a guarantee.

How this paper is built

  • Every question has a runner-up — an option that would be correct if one fact in the question were different. That fact is stated once, in the middle of the question, and not highlighted.
  • All options are about the same length, and the right answer is rarely the longest. This is checked automatically on every question.
  • Options state an action, never a reason. Wording like "so that", "because", "always" or "never" does not appear in any option.
  • Topic tags stay hidden until the question is graded.

When an answer is revealed you see the deciding fact, and the runner-up is marked in amber. The final report tells you how many of your wrong answers were the runner-up. If that is most of them, you know the material and the gap is in how you read the question. Before choosing, find the sentence that rules out the second-best option.
